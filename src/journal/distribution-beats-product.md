ModernClother was going to be simple. Find good apparel, put it online, sell it. I had the store, the catalog, the photos. What I didn't have was a single clue about how anyone would actually *find* it.

## Where it went wrong

I priced for margin instead of for momentum. I assumed that a good product would market itself. Both assumptions were expensive.

The painful realization: **product-market fit is mostly distribution-market fit in disguise.** A mediocre product with a great distribution wedge beats a great product nobody can find.

## What I do differently now

- I figure out the distribution channel *before* I build.
- I price for the behavior I want, not the spreadsheet I like.
- I treat the first 10 users as a research project, not a revenue line.

None of this is original. It's in every startup essay ever written. But there's a difference between reading "distribution beats product" and having it cost you a venture. The second one sticks.
