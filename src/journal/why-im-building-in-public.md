I've started nine things. Eight of them are paused, closed, or were lessons in disguise. For a long time I treated that as something to hide — a messy drawer you keep shut when guests come over.

This journal is me opening the drawer.

## The case for the open drawer

Building in private optimizes for looking competent. Building in public optimizes for *becoming* competent. Those are not the same goal, and for years I chased the first one.

> The next decade belongs to those who can bridge the gap between capital and code.

Every failed venture taught me something a polished case study would have hidden:

- **ModernClother** taught me that distribution beats product.
- **MediPro** taught me that B2B trust compounds slowly.
- **Arthhive** taught me that time is the scarcest resource.

## What this journal is

Not a highlight reel. A working log. I'll write entries as things happen — the dead-ends included — because the dead-ends are where the actual information lives.

If you're building something hard, I hope reading this makes your own drawer a little easier to open.
